# B1 — Official sources on tuberculosis notifications 2019-2023 (extracts and checks, 2026-10-11)

Purpose: support the Discussion paragraph on the period around the evaluation window, keeping the **observed changes** apart from the **causal
attribution made by the sources**. The manuscript does not test causes. Everything below was read in the source itself on 2026-10-11.

## 1. WHO, Global tuberculosis report 2024, section 2.1 "Case notifications"

URL: <https://www.who.int/teams/global-programme-on-tuberculosis-and-lung-health/tb-reports/global-tuberculosis-report-2024/tb-diagnosis-and-treatment/2-1-case-notifications>
(HTML downloaded and searched for each sentence; the sentences below were found verbatim.)

Observed (global, people newly diagnosed and notified; "new and relapse cases"):

- "The 8.2 million people newly diagnosed and notified with TB in 2023 is the highest number for a single year since WHO started to compile data from all countries and areas in the mid-1990s, up from the previous record of 7.5 million in 2022 and 15% higher than the pre-pandemic level of 7.1 million in 2019"
- "TB case notifications fell considerably below pre-pandemic levels, most noticeably in 2020 (with a global reduction of 18%, to 5.8 million) before a partial recovery in 2021 (to 6.4 million)."

Attribution made by WHO (kept separate in the manuscript):

- "These two years of record levels of TB case notifications follow two years of COVID-related disruptions to health services, when TB case notifications fell considerably below pre-pandemic levels"
- "It is likely that they also reflect two other factors: the diagnosis of a backlog of people who developed TB in previous years but whose diagnosis was delayed by COVID-related disruptions, and an estimated increase in the overall number of people developing TB disease"

Also consistent: WHO Global tuberculosis report 2021, section 3.1 (page "3.1 Case notifications"): "a substantial fall of 18% from 7.1 million in 2019";
cited only through the 2024 report to keep the bibliography short.

## 3. Limits

- WHO counts are global and include relapse cases; the Ministry's counts are new cases in Brazil. Neither is the supplied monthly series, whose level
  (annual mean 8,015 per month in 2019, i.e. about 96,000 a year) differs from the official new cases and whose extraction could not be recovered. The manuscript
  states that the direction of the changes is the same (annual means of the supplied series: -10.2%, +6.2%, +10.9% for 2020, 2021, 2022) and claims nothing about
  equality of the series.
- The later bulletin of March 2026 (data 2014-2025) reports 84,368 new cases in 2025, 2.1% fewer than in 2024; it is not cited because the evaluation window ends in 2022.
- The WHO page shows no publication date, so the reference gives the year of the report (2024) and the section.
