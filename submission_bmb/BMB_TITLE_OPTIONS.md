# Bulletin of Mathematical Biology — Candidate Title Options

This document evaluates five candidate titles across three distinct editorial framing families for submission to the *Bulletin of Mathematical Biology* (BMB), aligning with the journal's focus on mathematical modeling, practical identifiability, out-of-sample prediction, and biological application.

---

## Candidate Titles Evaluation

### Title Candidate 1 (Family A: Turning-Point Title)

* **TITLE**: Within-Family Error Reduction Versus External Forecasting Skill in Fractional-Order Compartmental Models: A Methodological Investigation of SEIT Tuberculosis Dynamics
* **FRAMING_FAMILY**: Family A (Turning-Point Title)
* **WHAT_EDITOR_SEES_IN_5_SECONDS**: An explicit mathematical-biology evaluation comparing within-family improvement against external forecasting benchmarks in compartmental epidemic models, tested on tuberculosis.
* **OVERCLAIM_RISK**: NONE. Does not claim superiority, novelty, or operational prowess; explicitly frames the central turning point.
* **BMB_FIT**: EXCELLENT. Matches BMB's interest in foundational methodology, model comparison rigor, and epidemiological dynamics.
* **RECOMMENDED**: YES (Primary Choice)

### Title Candidate 2 (Family B: Identifiability / Prediction Title)

* **TITLE**: Predictive Stability and Derived Threshold Robustness Under Practical Parameter Non-Identifiability in a Fractional SEIT Epidemic Model
* **FRAMING_FAMILY**: Family B (Identifiability / Prediction Title)
* **WHAT_EDITOR_SEES_IN_5_SECONDS**: A rigorous mathematical study on the coexistence of parameter non-identifiability with stable trajectory prediction and robust threshold/stability dynamics.
* **OVERCLAIM_RISK**: NONE. Focuses entirely on structural and dynamical properties over an admissible set.
* **BMB_FIT**: EXCELLENT. Parallels relevant BMB literature on prediction under poor identifiability (e.g., Simpson & Maclaren, 2024).
* **RECOMMENDED**: YES (Strong Alternative)

## Primary Selection

```text
BMB_PRIMARY_TITLE = "Within-Family Error Reduction Versus External Forecasting Skill in Fractional-Order Compartmental Models: A Methodological Investigation of SEIT Tuberculosis Dynamics"
```

### Rationale for Primary Selection:
1. Immediately presents the core mathematical-biology object (fractional-order compartmental models).
2. Explicitly highlights the central methodological turning point (within-family reduction vs. external forecasting skill).
3. Grounded in the specific epidemiological application (SEIT tuberculosis dynamics).
4. Completely eliminates the risk that a desk editor mistakes the manuscript for a standard "fractional model is superior" paper.
